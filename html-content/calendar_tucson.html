Tucson Sonoran Desert Garden Calendar

A month-by-month field calendar for a Tucson garden in the Sonoran Desert. The calendar emphasizes early identification and removal of common invasive or unwanted weeds, while highlighting native Sonoran Desert plants to retain, encourage, or plant.

The timing is approximate: rainfall, irrigation, elevation, and microclimate can shift emergence and flowering by several weeks. Do not remove an unfamiliar volunteer merely because it looks weedy; identify it first.

The weed-control recommendations are informed by University of Arizona Cooperative Extension, the Pima County Invasive Species Program, Arizona Invasive Plants, and Tucson Audubon. Native-plant choices are cross-checked against the 2026 Pima County Master Gardeners native-plant guide.

January

Remove / monitor

  • London rocket (Sisymbrium irio) — winter annual. Pull young rosettes and remove before seed production.
  • Sweet clover (Melilotus indica) — remove unwanted winter annuals while soil is moist and plants are small.
  • Stinknet (Oncosiphon pilulifer) — monitor newly emerging rosettes after winter rain; bag pulled plants.

Encourage / plant

  • Parry's penstemon (Penstemon parryi) — native Sonoran Desert spring wildflower; excellent for hummingbirds.
  • Desert willow (Chilopsis linearis) — native tree for appropriate wash-like/moister landscape positions.

February

Remove / monitor

  • London rocket (Sisymbrium irio) — often conspicuous and flowering now; pull before pods mature.
  • Stinknet (Oncosiphon pilulifer) — winter annual; remove flowering plants before seed set and bag them.
  • Sweet clover (Melilotus indica) — remove before flowering/seed production if unwanted.

Encourage / plant

  • Parry's penstemon (Penstemon parryi) — spring bloom approaches; protect seedlings and avoid confusing them with mustard rosettes.
  • Fairy duster (Calliandra eriophylla) — native shrub; a good time to establish container-grown plants before extreme heat.

March

Remove / monitor

  • London rocket (Sisymbrium irio) — pull before the long slender pods mature.
  • Stinknet (Oncosiphon pilulifer) — flowering is a critical control window; bag plants and do not let them seed.
  • Buffelgrass (Cenchrus ciliaris) — inspect landscaped and disturbed areas; remove before seed dispersal where feasible.

Encourage / plant

  • Parry's penstemon (Penstemon parryi) — major spring display.
  • Desert marigold (Baileya multiradiata) — native, drought-tolerant wildflower with a long bloom season.

April

Remove / monitor

  • Stinknet (Oncosiphon pilulifer) — remove before seed set; dried plants become a wildfire-fuel concern.
  • London rocket (Sisymbrium irio) — remove remaining flowering/fruiting plants before seed dispersal.
  • Buffelgrass (Cenchrus ciliaris) — remove seed-producing plants where practical.

Encourage / plant

  • Desert marigold (Baileya multiradiata) — flowering season begins strongly in Tucson.
  • Fairy duster (Calliandra eriophylla) — spring flowering native shrub.

May

Remove / monitor

  • Puncturevine / goathead (Tribulus terrestris) — remove very young plants before burs form.
  • Buffelgrass (Cenchrus ciliaris) — inspect before seed dispersal; cured grass is highly flammable.
  • Horse purslane (Trianthema portulacastrum) — warm-season annual in disturbed/irrigated areas; remove young mats if unwanted.

Encourage / plant

  • Desert willow (Chilopsis linearis) — native flowering tree; best in a landscape position with more available moisture.
  • Desert marigold (Baileya multiradiata) — native spring/summer wildflower.

June

Remove / monitor

  • Puncturevine (Tribulus terrestris) — remove before sharp seed burs mature.
  • Horse purslane (Trianthema portulacastrum) — remove young plants before they spread and seed.
  • Buffelgrass (Cenchrus ciliaris) — dried grass can become a major fire fuel; prioritize plants around structures and access routes.

Encourage / plant

  • Desert willow (Chilopsis linearis) — summer flowering native tree; provide appropriate supplemental water during establishment.
  • Chuparosa (Justicia californica) — native hummingbird shrub for hot, well-drained sites.

Remove / monitor

  • Palmer amaranth / carelessweed (Amaranthus palmeri) — summer annual; remove before mature seed production.
  • Puncturevine (Tribulus terrestris) — continue removing flowering and fruiting plants.
  • Horse purslane (Trianthema portulacastrum) — remove unwanted mats before seed production.

Encourage / plant

  • Saguaro (Carnegiea gigantea) — iconic Sonoran native; do not overwater established plants.

August

Remove / monitor

  • Palmer amaranth (Amaranthus palmeri) — remove before seed rain; mature plants can be very large.
  • Puncturevine (Tribulus terrestris) — collect burs and remove plants to prevent spread on shoes, tires, and animals.
  • Buffelgrass (Cenchrus ciliaris) — prioritize dry, cured plants near structures and pathways.

Encourage / plant

  • Chuparosa (Justicia californica) — native low-desert shrub and hummingbird resource.
  • Fairy duster (Calliandra eriophylla) — native shrub that responds to summer rain.

Remove / monitor

  • Puncturevine (Tribulus terrestris) — remove remaining seed-bearing plants and collect burs.
  • Palmer amaranth (Amaranthus palmeri) — remove before seed release.
  • Buffelgrass (Cenchrus ciliaris) — remove or manage dry stands, especially in defensible-space zones.

Encourage / plant

  • Fairy duster (Calliandra eriophylla) — native shrub with flowering after rainfall.
  • Desert willow (Chilopsis linearis) — native tree suitable for appropriate moist microhabitats.

November

Remove / monitor

  • London rocket (Sisymbrium irio) — new winter rosettes emerge after autumn/winter rain; pull while small.
  • Sweet clover (Melilotus indica) — remove unwanted rosettes before flowering.
  • Stinknet (Oncosiphon pilulifer) — monitor disturbed soil after rain; early removal is much easier than removing established seed-producing plants.

Encourage / plant

  • Parry's penstemon (Penstemon parryi) — sow/establish for spring display where appropriate.
  • Desert willow (Chilopsis linearis) — deciduous native tree; plant in suitable wash/rainwater-harvesting positions.

December

Remove / monitor

  • London rocket (Sisymbrium irio) — winter annual; easiest to remove at the rosette stage.
  • Sweet clover (Melilotus indica) — remove young winter plants if unwanted.
  • Stinknet (Oncosiphon pilulifer) — winter emergence can begin after rainfall; identify carefully and bag pulled plants.

Encourage / plant

  • Parry's penstemon (Penstemon parryi) — prepare for spring flowering.
  • Desert marigold (Baileya multiradiata) — native low-water wildflower; protect desirable volunteers.

Identification notes

  • London rocket can produce thousands of seeds; Arizona Invasive Plants recommends manual removal while young, at the basal-rosette stage.
  • Stinknet is a rapidly spreading invasive annual in southern Arizona. University of Arizona guidance emphasizes early detection, repeated control, and preventing seed spread. Do not burn it.
  • Buffelgrass and other invasive grasses can become serious wildfire fuels. Prioritize removal around structures and in defensible-space zones.
  • Puncturevine is easiest to manage before its characteristic spiny burs mature.
  • Palmer amaranth is native to Arizona, but it can still be an unwanted garden weed; "remove" here means remove where it is unwanted, not eradicate the native species from the landscape.
  • Many native Sonoran plants can appear spontaneously in a garden. Identify them before pulling them.

Disclaimer

This is a practical garden calendar, not a regulatory invasive-species list. For regulated or potentially hazardous plants, follow current Pima County, Arizona, and University of Arizona guidance.
